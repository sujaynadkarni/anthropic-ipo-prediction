How the Estimate Was Built

The steps, assumptions and sources behind the estimate.

By Sujay Nadkarni · Code on GitHub

The whole model is one line:

price per share = (multiple × 2027 revenue + cash − lease debt) ÷ shares

1. Read the S-1

The source is Anthropic's Form S-1, filed October 26, 2026. The price range and final share count are still blank and will arrive in an amendment. What matters most:

  • Quarterly revenue went from $0.38B (Q1 2025) to $11.56B (Q2 2026).
  • Gross margin is about 85% as reported, but about 45% if all compute is counted as a cost; the split isn't disclosed.
  • About $0.5 trillion of compute commitments with Broadcom, Google, Amazon, Microsoft and others.
  • 5–7% of recent revenue came from customers violating the usage policy.
  • A 10-for-1 stock split in September 2026; preferred shares convert 10:1.

2. Choose comparables

No public company looks like Anthropic. Its closest rival, OpenAI, is private; Google and Microsoft do too many other things. So the model uses OpenAI's pending $1.25 trillion round (about 10–12x forward revenue) as the anchor, checked against three public companies chosen for specific traits:

CompanyWhyEV / next-year revenue
PalantirAI premium~38x
NVIDIAAI growth at scale~8x
CoreWeaveCompute-heavy, debt-laden~3x

The model uses 10–15x: between NVIDIA and Palantir, trimmed for compute risk and because private-round prices run rich. xAI was excluded; its merger price implied about 500x a tiny revenue base.

3. Forecast 2027 revenue

Start from Q2 2026, cut Q3 by 6% once for the usage-policy enforcement, then grow at a steady quarterly rate. The current 2.4x pace would imply $3.7 trillion of 2027 revenue, which isn't possible. Compute sets the real limit: spreading each contract over its term gives about $100B of compute in 2027, enough for roughly $210B of revenue, or about 1.4x a quarter. That's the ceiling; 1.3x is the base case and 1.2x the stress case.

4. Adjust for cash and debt

  • Add $131.7B of cash and short-term investments.
  • Subtract about $115B for the Broadcom equipment leases ($161B over five years, discounted at 9%). Leases are debt in substance.
  • Don't subtract the cloud purchase deals. They're operating costs of the revenue already being valued, so counting them would double-count.

5. Divide by shares

About 16.9 billion fully diluted shares, inferred from a grant described as 5% of fully diluted shares. New shares sold for cash at a fair price (the IPO itself, Google's $10B option) don't lower value per share, so they're left out.

6. Apply the IPO discount

Banks typically price 10–15% below expected trading value so the stock pops on day one. Applied to the ~$110 base case, that gives an IPO range of $93–99.

Limitations

  • Revenue is a single steady growth rate, not a bottom-up forecast.
  • 2027 compute capacity is a straight-line estimate; contracts likely ramp more slowly.
  • OpenAI figures come from press reports and conflict with each other.
  • No explicit discount for the founders' 50.1% voting control.

Data & tools

SEC EDGAR (S-1), yfinance (peer data), press reports from CNBC and Bloomberg (OpenAI, xAI). Model written in Python. Full source and README on GitHub.

Not investment advice. An educational exercise built on the author's assumptions with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, the company being valued.
